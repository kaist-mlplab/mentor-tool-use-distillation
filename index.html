MENTOR: Reinforcement Learning via Flexible Teacher-Optimized Rewards for Tool-Use Distillation

Distilling LLM tool use into small language models with on-policy RL and set-based teacher-alignment rewards

EMNLP 2026

Abstract

Distilling the tool-use capabilities of large language models (LLMs) into small language models (SLMs) is essential for their practical application. The predominant approach, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), is an off-policy distillation method that suffers from poor out-of-domain (OOD) generalization because it rigidly aligns with static teacher trajectories. While reinforcement learning (RL) offers an alternative, the capacity limitations of SLMs pose a severe dilemma: sparse outcome rewards provide insufficient guidance, whereas strict trajectory matching imposes overly restrictive constraints. To bridge this capacity-driven gap, we propose MENTOR, an on-policy distillation framework that introduces a flexible yet process-aware reward structure. Instead of enforcing rigid replication, MENTOR uses the teacher’s reference to guide tool-use behavior, balancing behavioral alignment with downstream performance. Extensive experiments on controlled executable-tool benchmarks demonstrate that MENTOR improves OOD tool-use performance compared to SFT and strict RL baselines. Our findings suggest that within verifiable tool-use environments, flexible tool-use alignment offers a more effective approach than strict trajectory replication for developing adaptable small models.

Method

Small models trained with outcome-only rewards learn to avoid tools, while strict trajectory-matching rewards (as in ToolRL) make them call tools but steadily accumulate invalid calls. MENTOR (Model Enhancement by Non-rigid Teacher-Optimized Rewards) sits between these extremes: it uses the teacher’s trajectory as a reference for which tools to use, not as a sequence to copy.

  1. Teacher references. A teacher (Qwen3-235B-Thinking) solves each training question, producing a trajectory τ(t)=⟨(r1,t1,o1),…,(rLτ,tLτ,oLτ)⟩\tau^{(t)} = \langle (r_1, t_1, o_1), \ldots, (r_{L_\tau}, t_{L_\tau}, o_{L_\tau}) \rangleτ(t)=⟨(r1​,t1​,o1​),…,(rLτ​​,tLτ​​,oLτ​​)⟩ of reasoning, tool-use and observation steps and an answer y^(t)\hat{y}^{(t)}y^​(t). Only trajectories whose answer matches the ground truth are kept.
  2. On-policy rollouts. The student generates a group of rollouts O(s)O^{(s)}O(s) for the same question, executing its Python tool calls in a sandbox.
  3. Composite reward. Each rollout is scored with R(O(s),O(t))=wcRc+waRa+wvRvR(O^{(s)}, O^{(t)}) = w_c R_c + w_a R_a + w_v R_vR(O(s),O(t))=wc​Rc​+wa​Ra​+wv​Rv​ where Rc = 1 if the final answer matches the teacher’s answer; the teacher-alignment reward Ra = 1 if the student used exactly the same set of tools as the teacher, {t∣t∈τ(s)}={t∣t∈τ(t)}\{t \mid t \in \tau^{(s)}\} = \{t \mid t \in \tau^{(t)}\}{t∣t∈τ(s)}={t∣t∈τ(t)}, regardless of order or count; and the tool-validation reward Rv = 1 only if every tool call in the student’s trajectory executes without error.
  4. Policy update. The student is optimized with GRPO: rewards are normalized within each rollout group into advantages that update the student policy.

Training uses mathematical reasoning (AceReason-Math) as a controlled domain. Students are Qwen2.5 (1.5B, 7B) and Qwen3 (1.7B, 8B). Baselines are the vanilla model, SFT on teacher trajectories, outcome-based RL, and Strict RL (a re-implementation of ToolRL’s trajectory-matching reward) in the same RL framework. In-domain evaluation uses six math benchmarks (Math-Forge, Omni-MATH, AIME24, AIME25, AMC23, MinervaMath); out-of-domain evaluation uses tools unseen in training: the BFCL-v4 function-calling benchmark and three retrieval QA benchmarks (Bamboogle, 2WikiMultiHopQA, HotpotQA). An alignment score (AS) based on the Jensen-Shannon divergence measures how close a model’s tool-usage distribution is to the teacher’s.

Main Results

  • Best in-domain accuracy at every scale. MENTOR reaches overall math accuracy of 48.26% on Qwen3-8B (SFT: 41.47%, Strict RL: 45.77%, vanilla: 39.12%; the 235B teacher: 52.06%), and 9.89% on Qwen2.5-1.5B (SFT: 3.17%, Strict RL: 6.95%).
  • Better out-of-domain tool use. On retrieval QA with unseen tools, MENTOR scores 20.73 EM on Qwen2.5-7B (SFT: 14.57, Strict RL: 19.10) and 35.44 on Qwen3-8B (SFT: 34.14, Strict RL: 34.66). On BFCL-v4 it reaches 43.39 on Qwen3-8B (SFT: 42.16, Strict RL: 42.95).
  • SFT barely transfers. SFT gains little out of domain (e.g., BFCL-v4 overall on Qwen2.5-7B: 26.38 → 26.50), while MENTOR reaches 33.52.
  • Alignment tracks performance. In the reward ablation, the full MENTOR reward (Setting 6) gives the best Math (27.95), BFCL (31.52), RAG (21.08) and alignment score (88.63); across settings, performance and alignment score correlate with Pearson’s r = 0.85.
  • Fewer invalid calls. Configurations that include the tool-validation reward Rv drive the invalid tool-call rate to near zero during training, while those without it stay unstable.
